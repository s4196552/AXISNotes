# AXIS Suite — Product Research & Architecture Recommendations

**Research date: 30 September 2026 · Audience: AXIS product and engineering · Status: research and implementation input**

## 1. Executive assessment

AXIS should compete on a connected workflow: **organise your tools → collect knowledge → understand it → practise it → make time for it**, while keeping control over files and AI access.

The individual ingredients already have strong competitors. Rambox, Ferdium, and Wavebox organise web applications and accounts; Anki and RemNote schedule learning; DEVONthink, TagSpaces, and PhotoPrism offer local-model integrations. The opportunity is the combination, particularly explicit data permissions that follow content between applications. This is a positioning hypothesis, not a claim that no competitor offers a similar combination. Sources and product-specific comparisons follow below.

**Recommended first complete workflow:** In a Biology project, open study resources through Hub, take notes, generate reviewed flashcards, schedule the due workload, and reopen the source passage from a card. In a separate Medical project, restrict all AI processing—including OCR, embeddings, summaries, and derived cards—to approved on-device models.

### Updated requirements captured from your brief

| Area                 | Requirement                                                                                                                                                                                       |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| All six products     | Independent local desktop application, mobile app, and web application as the target product family. “App” is interpreted as iOS/Android; capability parity must be explicit rather than assumed. |
| AI everywhere        | Optional BYOK cloud providers and local AI in Hub, Notes, Deck, Calendar, Athena, and ULAP. Every product remains useful with AI disabled.                                                        |
| Hub organisation     | User-defined nested sections/folders containing websites, installed apps, games, files, and folders. Example: Gaming → Spotify website, Discord desktop app, VALORANT launcher.                   |
| Hub launch behaviour | Per-item choice of embedded session, default browser, chosen browser/profile, registered app, or local file handler. Multiple account shortcuts may share a URL while using separate sessions.    |
| Notes AI permissions | Explicit provider/model access per collection, folder, file, and selected content; separate permission to read, suggest edits, and apply changes.                                                 |
| Deck ↔ Calendar      | Spaced repetition controls card due dates; Calendar reserves time to do the reviews. These are different schedules.                                                                               |
| Existing promises    | Notes remains file-based. Athena and ULAP never modify, relocate, rename, or add sidecars alongside original source files.                                                                        |

### Evidence and limits

This is desk research from official product sites, documentation, company/investor announcements, and public repositories. It includes a read-only inspection of the local AXIS, Athena, and ULAP projects. Competitors were **not installed or benchmarked**. Marketing assertions are not treated as independent performance evidence. Suggested improvements below are our product judgments, not verified defect reports.

Technology means the components publicly evidenced in the cited material, not a complete infrastructure inventory. “Not verified” means the evidence reviewed did not establish the fact; it does not mean the feature or funding does not exist. Funding rounds are historical disclosures, not current cash balances, valuations, or estimates of total funding today. No application code was changed for this report.

## 4. Hub: custom browsers, local files, and multiple accounts

### Feasibility verdict

**Feasible on desktop, with different mechanisms for different targets.** Windows WebView2 supports profile separation, and Chromium documents launching with a specified user-data directory. These establish viable building blocks, not a guarantee that every website/login works in a Tauri webview. [WebView2 profiles](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/webview2/concepts/user-data-folder) · [Chromium profiles](https://www.chromium.org/developers/creating-and-using-profiles/) · [Tauri webview configuration](https://v2.tauri.app/reference/config/)

Recommended item types are **website, installed application, game/launcher, file, folder, and AXIS resource**. Each item has a display name, icon, section, and launch configuration. Folder organisation and browser-session identity must be separate: moving a Facebook shortcut from Personal to Work must not change its login session.

| Example                      | Stored intent                                   | Behaviour                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Gaming / Spotify             | Website + chosen opening mode                   | Open in its saved Hub session or selected browser.                                         |
| Gaming / Discord             | Registered desktop app or selected shortcut     | Launch/focus the installed app; retain the website as an optional separate entry.          |
| Gaming / VALORANT            | User-selected installed launcher/shortcut       | Use the installed launch mechanism; no guessed executable path or anti-cheat manipulation. |
| Social / Facebook — Personal | Facebook URL + Personal browser/session profile | Preserve that account’s cookies independently of other shortcuts.                          |
| Social / Facebook — Work     | Same URL + Work profile                         | Separate authenticated session; never copy cookies or store Facebook credentials in AXIS.  |
| Study / Biology PDF          | Local file + preferred viewer                   | Open the original through its handler without importing or rewriting it.                   |

**Suggested implementation order:** native/file launching and external browser profiles first; embedded isolated sessions second. Use argument arrays and validated launch targets rather than building a shell command string. Do not share AXIS’s privileged webview with third-party websites. Add target testing, missing-app/file recovery, visible account labels, session removal, and inactive-tab suspension.

Embedded sessions should use AXIS-owned data storage. Launch existing browser profiles through the browser’s supported mechanism; do not point the embedded engine at a live Chrome/Edge profile directory. Test two accounts, browser-already-running behaviour, login popups, logout isolation, and restart persistence separately.

**Platform boundary:** A browser or phone cannot generally launch an arbitrary Windows executable. A web Hub may show the same shortcut and offer “Open on this computer” only through a paired desktop companion. Mobile uses supported app/universal links and platform document pickers. Tauri’s shell documentation limits mobile support to URL opening rather than arbitrary process spawning. [Tauri shell support](https://v2.tauri.app/plugin/shell/)

## 5. AI across the suite: access by provider, model, and source

### Product contract

Every app exposes the same understandable choices: **AI off; on-device model; explicitly trusted private server; BYOK cloud provider**. A private server on another machine is not labelled “on this device.” Model availability and modality matter: a text-only model does not satisfy a photo-captioning feature.

| Product  | Useful optional AI                                                                                        | Deterministic capability that remains available                           |
| -------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Hub      | Suggest sections, find a saved tool using natural language, summarise explicitly selected project context | Launching, sorting, shortcuts, project browsing                           |
| Notes    | Explain/rewrite selected notes, source-grounded answers, handwriting and diagrams                         | Editing, linking, templates, keyword search                               |
| Deck     | Draft cards, explain an answer, propose better questions                                                  | Card editing, review, spaced repetition                                   |
| Calendar | Parse planning requests and propose a schedule                                                            | Calendar editing, recurrence, conflict checks, rule-based time allocation |
| Athena   | Classify uncertain documents and draft source-linked summaries                                            | Deterministic extraction, catalogue, filters, search                      |
| ULAP     | Captions, semantic retrieval, selected visual/audio inference                                             | File catalogue, basic metadata, gallery, keyword filters                  |

### Your study/medical example

| Source                        | Example access policy                                                                                                                                    |
| ----------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Study notes                   | Allow a specifically selected OpenAI provider configuration and model, plus selected local alternatives; allow reading and suggestions.                  |
| Medical notes                 | Allow only explicitly approved models running on this device; deny cloud and private-server routes unless the user deliberately changes the policy.      |
| Passwords/private credentials | AI never; exclude from AI retrieval and generation context.                                                                                              |
| Mixed study + medical request | Intersect permissions across all sources. Use a model allowed for both, or ask the user to narrow the sources. Never drop restrictions through fallback. |

In the product UI, use **OpenAI API provider/model** for BYOK configuration rather than implying a ChatGPT subscription supplies API access. Opening ChatGPT as a Hub website is a separate use case; ChatGPT and API billing are separate. [OpenAI billing documentation](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/9039756-managing-billing-for-chatgpt-and-the-api-platform)

### Required changes to the current AXIS design

1. **Model-specific rules.** Extend the current `any/local/never` policy with allowed provider identities, endpoint identities, model IDs, and operation permissions. A task’s preferred model is a preference; a source’s allowed models are an access boundary.
2. **Restrictive composition.** Intersect applicable source restrictions. Conflicting restrictions produce a blocked request with a clear explanation. A permissive project membership must not override a restrictive source file.
3. **Filter before retrieval and transmission.** Authorise candidates before generating cloud embeddings, snippets, tool results, or prompts. Cloud “search” must not see medical text merely because the final answer is filtered.
4. **Carry provenance into derivatives.** A card, summary, OCR record, or cached excerpt derived from medical material inherits its disclosure restriction. A copy into a Study project is not automatic permission to disclose it.
5. **Separate reading from actions.** Model access does not imply permission to edit, delete, launch software, move calendar blocks, or share content. Preview proposed changes and record actual execution.
6. **Verify execution location.** A loopback URL is not proof of local inference. Ollama documents both cloud models and a mode that disables cloud features. Mark routing as unverified unless the connector can establish the configured model’s location; a strict on-device rule must fail closed. [Ollama FAQ](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/blob/main/docs/faq.mdx) · [Cloud behaviour](https://github.com/ollama/ollama/blob/main/docs/cloud.mdx)
7. **Make denied and unavailable different.** Explain whether a model is disallowed, not installed, incompatible, offline, or out of memory. Never silently substitute a cloud provider.
8. **Protect BYOK credentials.** Native clients use platform secure storage. Web clients require a deliberate direct-session or user-controlled gateway design; do not embed keys in static builds or quietly store them in general browser local storage. Document any server that receives a key.

**Important limit:** AXIS can enforce its own routing and supported connector checks. It cannot prove that arbitrary user-supplied proxy software never forwards data. Strict offline assurance needs a controlled local runtime and an egress-disabled verification scenario, not just a “local” badge.

## 6. Deck and Calendar: one learning workflow, two schedules

Use a proven FSRS implementation as the scheduling candidate. The upstream `ts-fsrs` project declares MIT licensing, making it a candidate for AXIS’s shared TypeScript learning core; adoption still requires a pinned version, dependency review, and parity tests. No dependency was added during this research. [Upstream package](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/ts-fsrs/blob/main/package.json)

**Deck owns:** card content, source references, review events, memory state, due times, scheduler version, and user retention preferences. AI can help write cards but must not invent review events or silently replace the scheduler.

**Calendar owns:** available time, fixed appointments, study-session allocation, recurrence, and reminders. It consumes a workload projection from Deck: deck/project, due count, estimated minutes, target window, and revision.

Example: Deck estimates 30 minutes of Biology reviews tomorrow. Calendar proposes 18:00–18:30. Moving that block to 19:00 leaves the cards’ learning state unchanged. Reviewing in Deck reduces the outstanding workload; cancelling the block leaves reviews due. The initial duration estimate should use a visible default, then learn from actual review timings.

Recommendations:

- Expose an idempotent workload update keyed by deck, date/window, and source revision; do not create duplicate calendar blocks on every sync.
- Preserve card history when a note changes. Show a source diff and let the user update, split, or retire a card.
- Make cards available offline, including required media. Preserve review events during sync and surface conflicts rather than silently discarding sessions.
- Treat exam preparation as an additional planning constraint, not a replacement for memory scheduling. RemNote explicitly describes this distinction. [Exam-scheduler reference](https://help.remnote.com/en/articles/9102040-understanding-the-exam-scheduler)
- Test DST, time-zone changes, missed days, overdue backlogs, suspended cards, and simultaneous mobile/desktop reviews. Use the same scheduler version and fixtures across clients.

| Calendar | Local planning and app integrations                              | Agenda, time blocks, notifications, offline changes                                | Planning and optional sync; offline cached operation                                                        |
| Athena   | Read-only source indexing and catalogue engine                   | Search approved catalogue data and scan selected accessible documents              | Search approved catalogue data through a paired/self-hosted service; browser-selected files where supported |
| ULAP     | Full staged processing over disk libraries                       | Gallery, review/search, selected-device media access and supported local inference | Gallery/search through a connected catalogue; supported browser-local inference for selected tasks          |

The web file-access APIs require permission and have browser-dependent support. They do not give a hosted site unrestricted access to the user’s disk. [File System API](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/File_System_API)

For AI, distinguish **on-device inference**, **private-computer inference**, and **cloud API inference** on every platform. Browser inference is feasible for supported models/devices through tools such as WebLLM, which requires WebGPU. This is a candidate capability, not evidence that every ULAP model or large language model can run on every phone. [WebLLM documentation](https://webllm.mlc.ai/docs/user/get_started.html)

**Engineering recommendation:** retain React/TypeScript domain/UI reuse and Tauri on desktop. Evaluate Tauri mobile against a small offline Notes/Deck slice before committing all six mobile clients. Keep Python extraction/ML engines on the desktop/private service initially; mobile on-device inference needs a separate supported runtime and model set. Preserve the requirement for mobile local AI, but publish a capability matrix rather than promising universal model parity.

## 9. Validation programme and next decisions

Recommended comparative trials before implementation choices become expensive:

1. **Hub session trial:** use two test accounts for the same site in isolated sessions, restart, log out of one, and verify the other survives. Compare with external browser-profile launching. Record login compatibility and resource usage rather than assuming a win over Electron.
2. **Privacy trial:** create Study and Medical fixtures. Request mixed-source answers through cloud, on-device, and cloud-forwarding local endpoints. Verify the actual outbound traffic as well as the visible message. Repeat with embeddings and generated cards.
3. **Learning trial:** replay identical review-event fixtures across desktop, web, and mobile. Compare due times and memory state; schedule and move Calendar blocks without changing them.
4. **Library trial:** index a representative file corpus containing duplicates, moved files, broken paths, corrupt media, and offline drives. Measure search latency, time-to-first-results, memory, and restart recovery. Verify source integrity.
5. **Portability trial:** export notes, cards plus review history, calendar events, and catalogue annotations. Restore them into a clean installation. Test local-only operation after disconnecting all online services.

Decisions that still need implementation-stage evidence: exact mobile runtime; supported on-device model set; browser/profile adapters by OS; the web BYOK credential path; and online deployment/sync design. They should be resolved by bounded prototypes, not by a claim that a shared frontend automatically gives feature parity.

**Recommended product emphasis:** build the smallest convincing private study workspace first, while making Hub’s mixed launcher independently useful. Keep Athena and ULAP as specialists, and make their source references and AI policies consistent with the rest of AXIS.
